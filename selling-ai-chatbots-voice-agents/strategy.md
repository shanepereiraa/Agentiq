# Marketplace Strategy — "Shane" Freelancer Identity (Fiverr + Upwork)

> Scope: the separate `@shanebuildsai` / "Shane" marketplace identity. NOT AgentIQ.
> AgentIQ stays premium, direct-sold, retainer-based, and never references Fiverr/marketplaces.
> This doc is about generating actual sales on Fiverr and Upwork under a deliberately separate brand.

---

## 1. The core diagnosis (why zero sales today)

The current Fiverr presence has **10 impressions / 1 click / 0 orders in 30 days**. That is not a pricing problem or a copy problem in isolation — it's an *offer + visibility* problem stacked on top of each other:

1. **The offer is weak in the buyer's eyes.** A "$50 custom AI chatbot with CRM and source code" reads as *too good to be true* → low perceived likelihood of a real result. Cheap price on a complex deliverable signals low quality, not value.
2. **The gig targets the wrong buyer.** "Perfect for SaaS companies & startups" contradicts the bio (restaurants/clinics/stores) and the validated AgentIQ ICP. SaaS founders build their own bots; restaurant/clinic/salon owners *pay* to have it done. Wrong audience = no clicks that convert.
3. **Zero trust signals.** No reviews, no gig video, unused description budget (788/1200), a title Fiverr itself flags as too long. Fiverr's algorithm has nothing to rank and no reason to surface it.
4. **Half the stated specialty has no product.** Voice agents are the higher-margin AgentIQ line (voice starts at ₹39,999 vs chatbot ₹24,999) and are named in the bio — but there is **no voice gig at all**. This is the single biggest gap.

The fix sequence, in order: **fix the offer → fix positioning → fix visibility/trust → add the missing voice offer → drive first reviews via warm outreach.**

## 2. Platform choice: Fiverr AND Upwork, with distinct roles

Run both, but they are not the same channel and should not be worked the same way.

| | **Fiverr** | **Upwork** |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer motion | Inbound — buyers search, you rank | Outbound — you send proposals to posted jobs |
| Core Four analog | "Posted content" (compounding SEO asset) | "Cold outreach" (you initiate) |
| Best for | Productized, repeatable builds (chatbot gig) | Consultative, higher-ticket, retainer-friendly (voice agents) |
| No-review penalty | **Severe** — new sellers get buried until 3-5 reviews | **Mild** — a sharp tailored proposal wins even at 0 reviews |
| Retainer/recurring | Via native Gig Subscriptions (ToS-compliant) | Native ongoing hourly/retainer contracts (encouraged) |
| Time to first sale | Slow (weeks; needs reviews or paid Promoted Gigs) | Fast (days, if proposals are good) |

### Recommendation — where to apply the Rule of 100 first

**Lead with Upwork as the active sales channel; let Fiverr bake as the compounding storefront.**

Reason: a brand-new seller with 0 reviews gets fastest first-paid-work + first reviews on Upwork, because Upwork buyers read a tailored proposal directly and can hire on proposal quality alone. Fiverr, by contrast, will not surface an unreviewed gig no matter how good the copy is — it needs seed reviews before the flywheel turns.

So:
- **Upwork = the Rule of 100 channel now.** Target: 10-15 tailored proposals/day (the realistic Connect-budget-bound analog of "100 activities"). This is where sales come from in weeks 1-8.
- **Fiverr = optimize once, then feed it.** Fix both gigs (below), add a video, then *route your first 2-3 warm/network buyers through Fiverr* to seed reviews. Once 3-5 reviews exist, Fiverr's algorithm starts surfacing the gigs and the inbound flywheel compounds for free.

Do **not** spread thin across four channels. One active channel (Upwork proposals) dominated first; Fiverr as the parallel asset that pays off later. Add nothing else until one of these is producing consistent orders.

## 3. Marketplace pricing — what it SHOULD be

### The reference point
AgentIQ chatbot Starter = ₹24,999 setup (~$300) + ₹6,999/mo (~$85/mo). That's the *validated* worth of the underlying capability. Current Fiverr Basic ($50) is ~6x below just the AgentIQ *setup fee*, with no recurring component at all — a ~25x total gap. That underpricing actively signals "amateur" and attracts the worst clients.

### The reasoning for marketplace pricing
- **Discount the setup fee, don't gut it.** Marketplace buyers are more price-sensitive, Fiverr takes 20%, and you're building trust with no reviews. A ~30-40% discount off AgentIQ's setup pricing is defensible. Racing to $50 is not.
- **Anchor with a credible floor.** The Basic tier should still be priced like real work ($120-150), not a loss-leader. Cheap-but-complex = distrust.
- **Use tiers to sell up, not to sell cheap.** Basic = a genuinely narrow deliverable (single-channel FAQ + lead capture). The value climbs steeply at Standard/Premium where CRM, booking, multichannel, and source code live.

### Recommended Fiverr pricing

**Chatbot gig** (revised from $50/$120/$250):
| Tier | Price | Maps to |
|------|-------|---------|
| Basic — Starter Bot | **$135** | ~AgentIQ Starter setup, discounted, single channel |
| Standard — Lead Machine | **$325** | + CRM/Sheets + qualification + booking link |
| Premium — Growth Suite | **$650** | Multi-channel + booking + handoff + multilingual + source + 30-day support |

**Voice agent gig** (new — higher floor; voice is harder and higher perceived value):
| Tier | Price | Maps to |
|------|-------|---------|
| Basic — Inbound Voice Starter | **$275** | 1 inbound FAQ voice agent, Hi+En, setup only |
| Standard — Booking Voice Agent | **$550** | + appointment booking + CRM/Sheets call logging + call summaries |
| Premium — Voice Suite | **$1,000** | In+outbound, multilingual, custom voice, up to 3 outlets, 30-day support |

**Extras (drive AOV up):** 3-day express +$60 · extra channel +$90 · extra language +$45.

> **Voice-specific honesty note:** voice agents carry ongoing platform costs (Vapi + telephony/Twilio minutes) that a one-time gig can't absorb. Gig price = **build/setup only**; the buyer supplies their own Vapi + phone number, OR takes the monthly management subscription. State this plainly in the gig so there are no refund disputes.

### The recurring-revenue bridge (ToS-safe)
Fiverr one-time gigs have no retainer — but **Fiverr Gig Subscriptions are native and ToS-compliant.** Add a monthly maintenance/optimization offering:
- **"Monthly Bot Care" subscription — $80/mo:** content updates, prompt tuning, monthly performance check, priority fixes.
- This is the on-platform analog of AgentIQ's ₹6,999/mo retainer. It converts one-time buyers into MRR *without* violating Fiverr's rules.
- On Upwork, do this the native way: convert the fixed-price build into an **ongoing hourly/retainer contract** for maintenance — fully encouraged by the platform.

> **ToS guardrail:** Never solicit off-platform payment (WhatsApp/UPI/bank) on Fiverr or Upwork for work sourced there. It's the fastest way to get banned. Use Fiverr Subscriptions and Upwork ongoing contracts for recurring revenue. AgentIQ direct clients are a *separate* pipeline — keep the two worlds cleanly apart, which the existing brand-separation decision already enforces.

**Drop "SaaS companies & startups" entirely.** Replace with the validated ICP, phrased for marketplace search intent:

This aligns the gig with the bio, the AgentIQ ICP, and — critically — buyers who *pay for done-for-you* rather than build it themselves. Keep the outcome language buyer-facing: not "I build chatbots," but "you stop losing leads while you sleep."

## 5. Fixing the existing chatbot gig (visibility + trust)

Concrete fixes, not "improve SEO":

1. **Shorten the title** from 66 chars to ~45-50, front-loaded keyword. Fiverr's own UI flagged the length. (New copy in `gigs/fiverr-chatbot-revised.md`.)
2. **Use the full description budget** — 788/1200 leaves 400+ chars of persuasion on the table. Add proof-of-process, "how it works" steps, and a guarantee.
3. **Fix the delivery inconsistency.** Bio says 3-day, gig shows 6-day. Pick **5-day standard** (realistic for a solo builder on an M1/8GB) and sell 3-day as a paid express extra. Make the bio match.
4. **Add a gig video.** Fiverr boosts gigs with video materially. A 45-60s screen-recording of a working demo bot answering a customer question does more than any copy.
5. **Add 2-3 gig images** showing a real demo bot (clearly labeled "demo," never faked client work). Screenshots of the chat in action.
6. **Add a strong guarantee** to lift perceived likelihood: *"Working bot delivered or your money back — full refund if it doesn't do what we agreed."* Costs little at these volumes and directly attacks the buyer's #1 fear.
7. **Tag correctly.** Keep the chatbot tags; the voice tag (Vapi.ai) belongs on the *new* voice gig, not this one.
8. **Seed the first reviews via warm outreach.** Invite 2-3 people from Shane's network (or a past favor) to order a Basic build at a genuine price and leave honest reviews. This is the Core Four "warm outreach first" principle — it's what breaks the 0-review deadlock and unlocks Fiverr's algorithm. (Must be real orders for real work; do not fake reviews — Fiverr detects and bans this.)

## 6. The marketplace "lead magnet" — the free personalized Loom

Buyers on both platforms message before they buy. Convert those inquiries with a genuine value-first move:

> When a prospect messages, reply with a **2-3 min personalized Loom/screen recording**: "Here's exactly how a bot would answer *your* customers" — using their real business name and 2-3 of their actual FAQs.

This is a "solve-a-problem" lead magnet: the buyer gets a tangible taste of the result before paying. It dramatically out-converts a text reply, costs 5 minutes, and works identically on Fiverr inbox and Upwork proposals. This is the highest-leverage conversion tactic once traffic exists.

## 7. Honesty constraint (no fake track record)

There are **0 orders and 0 reviews** today. All copy must stay honest:
- Where useful, *lean into* new-seller status — "New on Fiverr and taking on a limited number of foundation clients at a special rate" — which frames low price as a launch offer, not low quality, and adds urgency.
- No invented testimonials, no fake client logos, no "trusted by 50+ businesses."
- Demos must be labeled as demos.
- The guarantee does the trust-building work that reviews will do later.

## 8. Sequence summary (offer → magnet → nurture → channel → amplifier)

1. **Offer:** re-priced, re-tiered chatbot gig + brand-new voice gig, both with a money-back guarantee and a maintenance subscription for MRR.
2. **Magnet:** free personalized Loom on every inquiry.
3. **Nurture:** Fiverr Subscription / Upwork ongoing contract as the retainer bridge.
4. **Channel:** Upwork proposals as the Rule-of-100 active channel now; Fiverr as the compounding storefront seeded with warm-outreach reviews.
5. **Amplifier:** once 3-5 reviews exist and delivery is smooth, ask every happy buyer for a review (Fiverr) and a "top-rated" nudge (Upwork). Referrals come after the offer visibly converts — not before.

**Single highest-priority next action:** publish the new **voice agent gig** (`gigs/fiverr-voice-agent-new.md`) — it's the biggest gap, the higher-margin line, and it doesn't exist yet.
